Purcell enhancement in layered InSe on the Mie-resonant silicon nitride waveguide
A.I. Veretennikov1, E.A. Shepelev2, A.S. Shorokhov2, P.A.
Alekseev1, I.A. Eliseyev1, A.A. Fedyanin2, and M.V. Rakhlin1∗
1Ioffe Institute, 194021 St. Petersburg, Russia
2Faculty of Physics, Lomonosov Moscow State University,
119991 Moscow, Russia
(Dated: May 12, 2026)
Hybrid integration of layered van der Waals (vdW) semiconductors with dielectric resonant struc-
tures provides an effective approach for controlling excitonic emission dynamics. Here, we demon-
strate Purcell-enhanced spontaneous emission from a thin InSe flake integrated with a Mie-resonant
Si3N4 waveguide. The structure is designed to spectrally overlap with the InSe photoluminescence
band and enhance coupling of excitonic emission to the guided mode. The micro-photoluminescence
measurements confirm enhanced waveguide transmission in close vicinity of the predefined optical
resonance. Time-resolved photoluminescence shows a reduction of the excitonic decay time by up to
a factor of three relative to planar InSe. The extracted Purcell factors are approximately 3 for out-
of-plane excitons and 2.1 for in-plane excitons. These results demonstrate resonator-induced control
of excitonic recombination in layered InSe and highlight vdW–dielectric interfaces as a platform for
integrated excitonic and quantum photonic devices.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Atomically thin van der Waals (vdW) semiconductors
have emerged as a promising platform for next-generation
photonic and optoelectronic devices owing to their unique
electronic properties and mechanical flexibility [1, 2].
However, their practical integration into on-chip photonic
circuits remains hindered by inefficient coupling of lumi-
nescence to guided photonic modes, which often arises
from random excitonic dipole orientations and ultrafast
radiative dynamics [3–5]. Among layered materials, III-
VI metal chalcogenides such as InSe offer distinctive ad-
vantages. They exhibit radiative lifetimes as long as 15 ns
[6, 7] and predominantly possess an out-of-plane (OP) ex-
citonic dipole moment orientation [8, 9]. These properties
make them inherently suitable for efficient coupling to
the fundamental modes of conventional dielectric waveg-
uides [10, 11]. However, experimental implementations of
2D vdW emitters integrated with conventional waveguide
structures have so far demonstrated light-matter cou-
pling efficiencies typically limited to the range of 5–15%
[12, 13], while the operation speed remains limited by the
relatively long radiative decay time [14, 15]. A promising
route toward the simultaneous enhancement of coupling
efficiency and operation rate in vdW-dielectric devices
is the integration of low-loss resonant nanostructures in
close proximity to the emitter [16, 17]. This approach en-
ables the Purcell effect to be harnessed, offering not only
enhanced spontaneous emission rates but also directional
emission into desired optical channels. For operation in
the near-infrared spectral range, compatible with mod-
ern quantum information technologies, Si3N4 represents
a particularly favorable waveguide material owing to its
high transparency, low absorption losses, and mature fab-
rication technology [5, 18, 19]. While recent studies on

this end, a thin InSe flake was transferred onto a resonant
Si3N4 waveguide incorporating Mie-type nanoparticles.
The waveguide geometry was designed to enhance the
coupling of excitonic emission from the InSe layer into the
guided mode. Micro-photoluminescence (µ-PL) measure-
ments reveal an increase in the collected emission inten-
sity, attributed to improved coupling of the emitted light

II.
NUMERICAL CALCULATIONS
The structure under study is a silicon nitride reso-
nant waveguide (RW) system shown in Figure 1. The
RW structure consists of two distributed Bragg reflec-
tors and a resonant cavity covered with a 12 nm thick
InSe film.
The cavity is implemented as a chain of
resonant nanoparticles whose thickness decreases from
the edges toward the center of the chainn following a
parabolic profile. Such a spatially varying geometry in-
duces a parabolic distribution of the effective refractive
index within the structure, thereby enabling efficient op-
tical field localization inside the resonator [21].
To identify the optimal geometrical parameters of
the resonant waveguide, numerical simulations were car-
ried out using the finite-difference time-domain (FDTD)
method. Based on the simulation results, the following
waveguide parameters were adopted: waveguide width
W = 0.85 µm, height H = 0.4 µm, nanoparticle period
Λ = 0.3 µm, cavity length Lcav = 6 µm, and Bragg re-
flector length Lref = 3 µm. The thickness of the nanopar-

c) Optical image of the transferred flake on the RW
ticles within the cavity was varied from l = 150 nm to
l = 170 nm.
For this configuration, the Purcell factor and the cou-
pling to the guided mode were calculated (Figure 2). Cal-
culated values are expressed relative to those of a conven-
tional waveguide (CW) with identical geometrical prop-
erties. For the calculation, an electric dipole source with
an out-of-plane orientation was placed on top of the film.
Calculations were performed for various positions of the
dipole source along the resonant cavity, after which the
obtained results were averaged. The results in Figure 2
confirm the presence of a high-Q resonance within the
given optical range (927 nm). From the simulation the
optical coupling efficiency of the resonant waveguide at
the target wavelength was found to be 7 times higher
than that of the conventional strip waveguide, and the
Purcell factor to be 2.5 times higher.
III.
SAMPLES AND FABRICATION
A.
Waveguide structures etching
Experimental samples based on a silicon nitride waveg-
uide system were fabricated using the following methods:
1. A silicon wafer was placed in a furnace at high tem-


3
perature in an oxygen atmosphere, resulting in the
formation of a layer of silicon dioxide (SiO2) with
a thickness of 2 µm on the silicon surface .
2. A film of 0.4 µm thick silicon nitride (Si3N4)
was deposited onto the oxide layer by low-pressure
chemical vapor deposition (LPCVD).
3. The mask with the desired geometry was patterned
on the sample by electron-beam lithography.
4. Reactive ion etching was performed through the ob-
tained mask. During this process, the material was
removed from the areas not protected by the mask
down to the SiO2 layer.
After etching was com-
pleted, the remaining mask residues were removed
by selective chemical etching.
The SEM images of the etched resonant structure are
presented in Figure 3. The geometrical properties of the
RW closely match those obtained from the numerical sim-
ulations. In addition to the resonant waveguides, a con-
ventional strip waveguide was fabricated with the same
height and width as the resonant one, serving as a refer-
ence.
B.
InSe flakes preparation
Two InSe flakes with comparable thicknesses of ∼13
nm (16 monolayers) were prepared using a dry-transfer
technique.
The selected thickness simultaneously pro-
vides an intense PL signal [7] and efficient coupling of
OP exciton emission with the resonant mode of the RW
[20]. The flakes were then placed sequentially at the cen-
ter of the RW and CW structures. The optical image of
the RW structure with the transferred flake is shown in
Fig. 3c. Atomic force microscopy topography confirms
the surface uniformity of the flakes over the entire area,
and additional µ-Raman mapping across the full active
region shows no qualitative or quantitative differences
relative to planar InSe within the experimental uncer-
tainty.

VI.
MEASUREMENTS OF LUMINESCENCE
KINETICS
The pronounced optical resonance supported by the
RW structure is expected to affect not only the coupling
efficiency but also the recombination dynamics of exci-
tons whose emission is coupled to the resonant mode.
To clarify the role of the RW resonances in the recom-
bination process, we performed time-resolved photolumi-
nescence (TRPL) measurements and extracted the cor-
responding PL decay times. In all TRPL experiments,
background signals not associated with InSe emission
were suppressed by selecting the spectral range of 920–
960 nm.
Figure 6a shows normalized TRPL curves measured
for an InSe flake on a planar substrate and for lumines-
cence coupled from the flake into the RW and collected
from the output grating. The decay curve of the guided
emission (red) exhibits a markedly faster PL decay than
that of the planar reference (blue). The same trend is
observed for all probed positions on the planar region

Within this framework, the recombina-
tion rate is enhanced for the emission channel coupled to
the resonant mode, whereas emission into weakly cou-
pled channels may exhibit an effectively reduced rate
due to the redistribution of the local density of opti-
cal states [24, 25]. This interpretation is supported by
the TRPL signal measured from the RW region in the
back-scattering geometry (green curve in Fig. 6a), which
shows a slower decay than the planar reference.
The
same mechanism also explains the enhanced guided PL
intensity observed for the RW structure, as discussed in
the previous section. To verify that the observed lifetime
modification originates from the resonant elements of the
RW, we performed identical TRPL measurements on the
CW structure using another InSe flake of similar thick-
ness. No statistically significant changes in the PL decay
times were observed for the CW structure, confirming the
essential role of the resonator in the lifetime reduction.
Although the decay curves clearly indicate a reduc-
tion of the PL lifetime, a quantitative estimation of the
Purcell factor requires identification of the independent
recombination channels involved in the coupling pro-
cess.
In a recent study [7], the PL decay of InSe was
decomposed into ”fast”, ”middle”, and ”slow” compo-
nents associated with direct interband recombination,
indirect phonon-assisted interband recombination, and
defect-related recombination, respectively. Following this
approach, we fitted all decay curves with a sum of three
exponential functions, as described in Ref. [7]; the result-
ing fits are shown as solid lines in Fig. 6a. For all TRPL
curves, the lifetime of the ”slow” component exceeded
100 ns, and this contribution was therefore excluded from
the subsequent analysis. The extracted ”fast” and ”mid-
dle” lifetimes are summarized in Fig. 6b. The ”fast” com-
ponent exhibits a substantially stronger reduction than
the ”middle” component, which we attribute to differ-
ent coupling efficiencies of the corresponding excitonic
recombination channels to the resonant mode.
In line with Ref. [7], the ”fast” and ”middle” compo-
nents are predominantly associated with recombination
of OP and IP excitons, respectively. Band-structure cal-
culations for layered InSe [9] indicate that the oscillator
strength of OP excitons is significantly larger than that of
IP excitons, implying intrinsically shorter radiative life-
times for OP exciton recombination. Moreover, in the
resonant waveguide geometry, OP excitons are expected
to couple more efficiently to the magnetic-dipole (MD)
mode, leading to a stronger Purcell enhancement for OP
emission than for IP emission. From the measured life-
time reductions, we estimate Purcell factors of approxi-
mately 3 and 2.1 for OP and IP exciton recombination,
respectively.
VII.
CONCLUSION
We
have
experimentally
demonstrated
Purcell-
enhanced
spontaneous
recombination
in
a
hybrid
vdW-dielectric structure consisting of a thin InSe flake
integrated with a resonant Si3N4 waveguide incorporat-
ing a Mie-type nanoparticle array. The optical resonance
of the waveguide was spectrally matched to the InSe
emission band, enabling enhanced coupling of excitonic
emission to the guided mode. Using µ-PL spectroscopy,
we
observed
an
increase
in
the
waveguide-coupled
emission intensity in close vicinity to the predefined
resonance for the resonant waveguide compared with a
conventional strip waveguide, predicted by numerical
simulations. Time-resolved photoluminescence measure-
ments revealed a reduction of the PL decay time by up


6
to a factor of three relative to a planar InSe reference.
By decomposing the decay kinetics into fast and middle
components associated predominantly with out-of-plane
and in-plane excitons, respectively, we estimated Purcell
factors of approximately 3 for out-of-plane excitons and
2.1 for in-plane excitons.
Our results provide direct experimental evidence of
Purcell-enhanced spontaneous recombination in layered
InSe coupled to a dielectric resonant waveguide. These
findings establish hybrid vdW-dielectric resonant inter-
faces as a versatile platform for engineering radiative de-
cay rates in layered semiconductors. This approach opens
a route toward high-speed on-chip quantum light sources,
excitonic modulators, and efficient integrated emitters for
nanophotonic and quantum photonic applications.
