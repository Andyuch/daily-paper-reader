Abstract 

For periodic photonic structures with full permittivity and permeability tensors and arbitrarily oriented optical axes, we present, to the best of our knowledge, the first anisotropic rigorous coupled-wave analysis framework that supports automatic differentiation and efficient GPU acceleration. The method formulates intralayer propagation as a boundary-value problem and directly constructs the scattering matrix using the precise integration method, thereby avoiding eigendecomposition of large non-Hermitian matrices. At comparable accuracy, the proposed method achieves a 25-fold speedup over the eigendecomposition-based approach. Its compatibility with automatic differentiation further demonstrates strong potential for topology optimization of anisotropic photonic structures. This framework provides an efficient computational tool for both forward analysis and inverse design of complex anisotropic devices. 

## 1. INTRODUCTION 

Periodic nanophotonic structures based on anisotropic media are increasingly important in flat optics, display optics, polarization control and tunable photonic devices 1-3 . Representative systems include liquid-crystal polarization gratings 3, birefringent metasurfaces 1, rotating-axis multilayers 4 and tensorial metamaterial gratings 5. Their optical response is governed not only by the unit-cell geometry, but also by the spatial distribution and orientation of the material tensors. Off-diagonal tensor components and optical-axis rotations introduce coupling between nominally orthogonal polarization channels and can substantially modify diffraction and polarization-conversion responses 6,7 . Accurate and efficient computational models are therefore essential for forward analysis, inverse design and topology optimization of anisotropic photonic devices. 

Rigorous coupled-wave analysis (RCWA) 8, also known as the Fourier modal method, is a standard electromagnetic solver for layered periodic structures 9,10 . Owing to its Fourier-basis representation and analytical treatment of propagation within individual layers, RCWA is often computationally advantageous for stratified periodic problems. However, eigen-decomposition in scattering-matrix (S-matrix) RCWA limits parallelization and becomes a bottleneck when the solver is accelerated on graphics processing units (GPUs) 11-13 . Early RCWA packages, including S4 9, RETICOLO 14 and GRCWA 15 , are primarily CPU-based frameworks. Recent GPU-accelerated eigen-decomposition schemes still suffer from the serial nature of eigen-decomposition 16,17 , and the gain becomes more limited when anisotropic media increase the size of the characteristic matrices. 

Gradient-based optimization is also central to inverse design of nanophotonic structures 18,19 . It updates design parameters from the gradient of a figure of merit (FOM) with respect to structural parameters. Among gradient-evaluation strategies, the adjoint method is attractive because it can optimize many parameters using one forward and one adjoint electromagnetic solve 20 , but problems involving many output quantities may require additional adjoint solves 21 . Automatic differentiation 22 evaluates the global gradient of the FOM through the chain rule and is supported by mainstream deep-learning frameworks 23 . Auto-differentiable RCWA frameworks based on eigen-decomposition, including TORCWA 16 , FMMAX 24 and GRCWA, have been reported, but they inherit difficulties associated with eigenvalue degeneracy. Eigen-decomposition-free propagation methods, including rigorous diffraction interface theory (R-DIT) 12 , matrix-exponential approximations 25 and matrix square-root formulations 13 , reduce computational cost and improve GPU compatibility by avoiding eigen-decomposition, and they are naturally compatible with automatic differentiation. However, most of these methods remain limited to specific material settings, such as isotropic media. Machine learning, neural networks and large language models are increasingly used in inverse design 26-29 , yet their performance for new materials or prescribed constraints still depends on training data generated by conventional simulation platforms. An efficient computational framework for periodic electromagnetic structures with general material tensors is therefore needed. 

The extension of RCWA to anisotropic media has a substantial theoretical basis 6,7,30,31 . Some RCWA frameworks, including S4 and FMMAX, already support electromagnetic simulation for selected uniaxial crystals. When the formulation is extended to biaxial crystals with arbitrary optical-axis orientations, the electric and magnetic fields couple more strongly. The characteristic matrix can then contain roughly four times as many elements as in the conventional formulation. For eigen-decomposition, this corresponds to at least an eightfold increase in computational cost. As a result, RCWA may lose part of its efficiency advantage over other numerical solvers for periodic structures, such as FEM, especially as these solvers continue to improve. Acceleration strategies that work well for isotropic media also become less applicable under anisotropy, because the electric-magnetic duality and homogeneity used in isotropic derivations deteriorate when material tensors are fully coupled. To our knowledge, no open-source implementation currently combines, in a single workflow, patterned in-plane variations of full complex 3 × 3 permittivity and permeability tensors, automatic differentiation with respect to tensor elements, stable S-matrix propagation and eigen-decomposition-free acceleration. 

Here we develop an open-source, differentiable and accelerated anisotropic RCWA framework in PyTorch for periodic structures with tensor-patterned layers. The key to differentiable acceleration is to apply the precise integration method to the boundary value problem, replacing the solution of the characteristic equation with basic matrix operations that can, in principle, reach machine precision. By applying the precise integration method to a two-point boundary value problem, the framework achieves approximately 25-fold acceleration on GPUs compared with eigen-decomposition-based propagation, and its formulation is not theoretically limited by layer thickness. We validate the method using lithium niobate metasurfaces, anisotropic multilayer films, and liquid-crystal Bragg polarization gratings. We also verify gradient calculations and demonstrate topology optimization with the model. This framework supports efficient development of anisotropic-material devices and training-data generation for inverse-design neural networks. It can also be integrated as a physical model in neural networks or used to construct intelligent agents with electromagnetic simulation capability through the Model Context Protocol (MCP).

Eigen-decomposition-based RCWA has a clear physical interpretation and is useful for resolving fields inside a layer. In many design problems, however, the quantity of interest is the overall electromagnetic response rather than the internal field distribution. In the S-matrix formalism, this response is represented by the global S-matrix. It is therefore inefficient to spend substantial computation on eigenmodes since the scattering matrix of each layer only requires the relation between the fields at its two boundaries. This point is particularly important for anisotropic layers. First, anisotropy introduces nonzero diagonal blocks into M, so the electric and magnetic fields that are decoupled in the isotropic derivation must be solved as a coupled system. The characteristic equation is then four times larger than that for an isotropic layer, which in theory increases the cost of eigen-decomposition eightfold. Second, separating forward and backward modes requires additional filtering. Machine precision and round-off error may affect the classification of low-frequency modes and can produce mismatched mode orderings across layers, although the final observables may be only weakly affected. Therefore, the following derivation focuses on the boundary value problem and solves the scattering matrix with the precise integration method 34 .

2.2. Precise integration method 

The solution of the first-order system in Eq. (6) requires a matrix exponential. For this problem, Moler and Van Loan reviewed 19 possible algorithms and noted that no single approach fully resolves all numerical issues 35 .Distinct from these 19 methods, the precise integration method was developed for matrix exponentials and aims to obtain a solution at computer precision. ## 3. RESULTS AND DISSCUSSION 

We validate the PIM-based RCWA solver using four groups of numerical tests. First, a lithium-niobate metasurface is used to assess accuracy, convergence with the number of truncated Fourier harmonics, computational performance, and stability with respect to layer thickness. A laterally homogeneous anisotropic multilayer then provides an independent benchmark against the transfer-matrix method. Transmissive and reflective liquid-crystal polarization Bragg gratings, whose permittivity tensors vary along both x and z, test the treatment of continuously varying volume structures. Since the FEM generally handles anisotropic media more effectively than the finite-difference time-domain method, most full-wave comparisons use COMSOL Multiphysics 6.4. Finally, automatic-differentiation gradients are compared with numerical gradients, and the solver is applied to topology optimization of anisotropic structures. In the following, for brevity, we refer to our method as PIM and the eigendecomposition-based method as EIG. 

Unless otherwise stated, the simulations use a PIM bisection order of N = 15, 361 truncated Fourier harmonics, and complex64 arithmetic with 32-bit real and imaginary components. The PIM solver is implemented in PyTorch and runs on an Intel Xeon Gold 6226R CPU and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU.

Recent work reports strong chirality in lithium-niobate nanofins induced by rotation of the optical axis 39 .Rotation of the extraordinary axis in the xy plane couples two orthogonal resonant modes. The resulting hybrid modes can form elliptically polarized states of opposite handedness, producing a strong circular-dichroism (CD) signal in an intrinsically anisotropic system. We define circular dichroism as CD = [T 𝑅𝐶𝑃 − T 𝐿𝐶𝑃 ]/[T 𝑅𝐶𝑃 +T𝐿𝐶𝑃 ], where T𝐿𝐶𝑃 , T 𝑅𝐶𝑃 are the co-polarized transmittances for left- and right-circularly polarized incidence, respectively. We use PIM to perform topology optimization from a random initial structure. The optimization produces a C2v-symmetric meta-atom with a pronounced chiral response. 

We first reproduce the co-polarized transmission spectra under normally incident x- and y-polarized light using the previously reported structural parameters, as shown in Fig. 6(a). The calculated 𝑇 𝑥𝑥 and 𝑇 𝑦𝑦 spectra reproduce the nearly degenerate resonance dips near 518 nm and their reported line shapes. In a fixed meta-atom, strong circular dichroism requires coupling between the orthogonal resonances. High-CD solutions are therefore sparse in the parameter space formed by the optical-axis angle and operating wavelength. For a fixed axis orientation and wavelength, manufacturable topologies that support the required mode coupling are likewise sparse in the much larger structural parameter space. To improve the flexibility of the parameter search ，we jointly optimize the optical-axis angle, operating wavelength, and freeform topology. 

The background material is air, and the solid material is anisotropic lithium niobate. Including material dispersion, the permittivity distribution is

## 4. CONCLUSION We develop a differentiable anisotropic RCWA framework for periodic structures with spatially patterned material tensors. The method avoids eigendecomposition of the large non-Hermitian matrices arising in anisotropic systems and eliminates the associated need for mode classification. Under typical Fourier truncation settings, it achieves a 25-fold speedup over the eigendecomposition-based approach. The inherent differentiability of the algorithm further enables topology optimization of photonic structures. Overall, the framework provides an accurate, efficient, and differentiable platform for modeling and inverse design of periodic electromagnetic structures with general permittivity and permeability tensors.

The RDIT algorithm provides a compact scattering-matrix formulation with high computational efficiency. For patterned layers thicker than half a wavelength, however, direct application of the algorithm becomes less robust. In principle, increasing the Taylor order improves the formal accuracy and can relax the thickness restriction. In practice, the nonzero off-diagonal terms introduced by anisotropic media break the symmetry between 𝕋 1, 𝕋 2 and further degrade the numerical conditioning of the matrices. The series order must therefore be high enough to maintain accuracy but not so high that numerical errors dominate in thick layers. In our tests, an excessive Taylor order can cause numerical divergence and produce results that violate energy conservation. 

This limitation can be mitigated in part by a binary slicing strategy similar to that used in PIM. The RDIT algorithm is first applied to a thin layer to obtain its scattering matrix. The thin-layer response is then doubled recursively through the Redheffer star product until the scattering matrix of layer i is obtained. The number of bisection steps cannot be increased without bound, because numerical errors in the thin-layer response can be rapidly amplified by the Redheffer star-product cascade. This issue is particularly important for resonant structures, where resonance features may be missed.
